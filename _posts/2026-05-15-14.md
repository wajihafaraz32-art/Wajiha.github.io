Database Management Systems turned out to be one of the most important subjects of my second semester, and in many ways, my experience with it mirrored what I had gone through with Programming Fundamentals the term before: a difficult start, followed by a deliberate push to improve, followed by a project that ended up teaching me more than the lectures alone ever could.

The course was taught by Dr. Bilal Ahmad, who had also been our instructor for Programming Fundamentals in the first semester. Going into DBMS, I think I assumed that having already adjusted to university-level coursework would make this subject easier to pick up. That assumption did not hold up in practice. Database concepts, while logically structured in their own way, required a different kind of thinking than what I had built up around programming logic, and I found myself struggling again, particularly around understanding how data should actually be organized and related within a system.

My midterm performance in DBMS reflected that struggle. The marks were not satisfactory, and I will admit that this felt discouraging in a familiar way, since I had already been through a similar disappointment with Programming Fundamentals the previous semester. But having gone through that experience once already gave me a clearer sense of how to respond this time. Rather than spiraling into doubt about whether I belonged in this field, I recognized the pattern: a difficult start that required a deliberate, focused response rather than passive worry.

So I started focusing more seriously on the subject, going back over core concepts more carefully and trying to build a stronger foundational understanding rather than just keeping pace with new material as it was introduced. This meant slowing down in some areas to make sure I actually understood the reasoning behind database design choices, rather than just memorizing structures without understanding why they worked the way they did.

The real turning point, though, was the DBMS project, which ended up being one of the most rewarding experiences of the entire semester. Through this project, I learned how to build a portfolio website and manage blog content within it, while also getting a much more practical, hands-on understanding of how database concepts actually apply in a real system rather than just in theory. There is a noticeable difference between understanding a concept on paper and actually implementing it in a working project, and this project was where that difference became clear to me.

I want to specifically acknowledge my group mate here, who supported me throughout this project and was genuinely helpful whenever I ran into difficulties. Group projects can be challenging in their own right, requiring coordination and shared responsibility, but having a reliable teammate made a significant difference in how smoothly this particular project came together, and I do not think I would have gotten as much out of the experience without that support.

By the time the project was finished, I felt like I had a much more concrete understanding of database systems than I had going into the course, not just academically, but practically. Building something real, even something as contained as a portfolio website, gave the underlying theory a kind of weight and relevance that lecture material alone had not fully provided.

In the final post of this series, I want to step back and talk about the broader lessons I have taken from this entire first year, across both Programming Fundamentals and Database Management Systems, and what I am hoping to carry forward into the rest of my degree.
