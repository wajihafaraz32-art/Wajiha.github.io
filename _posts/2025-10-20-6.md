There is a side of university life that does not get talked about as much as exams or projects, and that is the simple, exhausting logistics of just getting to and from campus every day. During my first semester, I was a day scholar, which meant I was commuting back and forth rather than living on campus, and I quickly learned that this came with its own distinct set of challenges that had nothing to do with the actual coursework.

Every day involved traveling long distances just to attend classes. By the time I got home in the evening, I was often genuinely exhausted, not from the academic content itself, but from the sheer physical toll of the commute layered on top of a full day of classes. This is not something I had fully anticipated before starting university. I think I assumed the hard part would be the studying, and while that was certainly true, the daily travel added a layer of fatigue that made everything else feel heavier than it might have otherwise.

Balancing studies, family responsibilities, assignments, and any kind of personal time became a real challenge during this period. There were days when I would get home with every intention of reviewing lecture material or working through a programming exercise, only to find that I simply did not have the energy left to focus properly. This created a kind of quiet pressure, because the coursework did not slow down just because my energy was running low, and I had to find ways to manage that gap.

I remember there being days when I felt extremely tired, the kind of tired that makes you question whether you have the stamina to keep this pace up for an entire degree. But I made a conscious decision to keep attending classes regularly regardless of how I was feeling physically, mainly because I did not want to fall behind, especially given how much effort I was already putting into catching up on the technical side of my coursework. Missing classes felt like it would only widen a gap I was already working hard to close.

Looking back, I think this period taught me something about discipline that I did not fully appreciate at the time. It is one thing to be motivated when you are well-rested and have a clear, energized mind. It is another thing entirely to keep showing up consistently when you are running on very little energy and the easier option would be to just stay home and rest. I am not saying I always made the optimal choice in hindsight, but the habit of consistency that I built during this time ended up mattering a great deal later in the semester, especially as deadlines and exams started to pile up.

This period also made me think more seriously about time management, although I will admit I did not master it as quickly as I would have liked. I was still learning to prioritize tasks, figure out what could wait and what could not, and accept that some days simply would not go as planned no matter how carefully I tried to schedule them.
