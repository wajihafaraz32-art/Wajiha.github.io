If my first semester taught me how to study with consistency, my second semester taught me how to manage competing priorities all at once, which turned out to be a completely different kind of challenge. The semester started with what felt like an unusually large number of things happening simultaneously, and looking back, I think that period was one of the more demanding stretches of my entire first year.

We had regular classes and assignments, as expected, but layered on top of that were midterm preparations, sports week activities, and Ramadan, all overlapping within roughly the same window of time. Individually, any one of these would have been manageable. Together, they created a kind of constant low-level pressure that made it difficult to ever feel like I was fully caught up on any single thing.

Managing everything at once was genuinely difficult, and I remember feeling overwhelmed more often during this period than I had during most of my first semester, even though by this point I had already been through one full term of university and theoretically should have felt more settled into the routine. I think this is an important thing to acknowledge honestly: just because you have successfully navigated one difficult period does not mean the next one will automatically feel easier. Each new combination of challenges can demand something slightly different from you.

Sports week, for example, brought a welcome change of pace from regular academic routine, but it also meant adjusted schedules and additional time commitments that had to be fit around existing coursework. Ramadan, meanwhile, changed daily routines in a more fundamental way, affecting energy levels and the timing of when I could realistically focus on studying. None of these things were negative in themselves, but trying to manage all of them simultaneously, alongside midterm preparation, required a level of planning that I had not fully developed yet.

What I learned during this period was less about any specific subject and more about the practical skill of prioritizing tasks. I had to get more honest with myself about what genuinely needed my attention on a given day versus what could reasonably wait. This was not always intuitive for me, and I made mistakes along the way, occasionally focusing on the wrong priority and having to scramble afterward to catch up on something I had pushed aside.

Looking back, I think this overwhelming start to the second semester ended up being a valuable, if uncomfortable, lesson in time management. It forced me to think more deliberately about how I structured my days, rather than simply reacting to whatever felt most urgent in the moment. That skill, more than any specific piece of subject knowledge from this period, has stayed useful well beyond this particular stretch of the semester.
