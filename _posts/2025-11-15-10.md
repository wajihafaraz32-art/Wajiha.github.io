University life is not only about exams and coursework, and I think it would be misleading to write about my first semester without mentioning some of the experiences outside the classroom that shaped it just as much. Two of those experiences stand out clearly when I look back: the welcome event early in the semester, and the Programming Fundamentals exhibition that came later on.

The welcome event happened fairly early in the semester, at a point when most of us were still getting comfortable with our new surroundings. It gave me a genuine opportunity to interact with classmates outside the more formal setting of a lecture hall. There is something different about getting to know people during a relaxed, social event compared to simply sitting next to them in class. Conversations flowed more naturally, and I think a number of friendships that carried me through the harder academic stretches of the semester actually started to take shape during this kind of informal interaction rather than in any classroom setting.

The Programming Fundamentals exhibition, on the other hand, was a completely different kind of experience, and honestly one of the more challenging events of the semester, even though it ended up being genuinely exciting as well. Unlike the welcome event, this was directly tied to our coursework, and it required actually applying what we had been learning in a more public, demonstrable way. I remember feeling a fair amount of pressure leading up to it, partly because Programming Fundamentals was already the subject I had struggled with the most, and partly because exhibitions, by nature, put your work in front of other people in a way that regular assignments do not.

What made the exhibition memorable, though, was not just the technical work involved, but the experience of working closely with classmates to pull everything together. I had to coordinate with other students, divide responsibilities, and rely on people outside of myself to get things done on time. This was a different kind of skill than anything I had needed for individual assignments, and it taught me a lot about the importance of clear communication and genuinely sharing responsibility rather than trying to control every part of a task myself.

I want to specifically mention our class representative here, who played a significant role in keeping different tasks organized during the lead-up to the exhibition. Coordinating an event like this across an entire class is not a small undertaking, and having someone willing to manage that logistical load made a real difference in how smoothly things came together. I think it is easy to overlook these kinds of contributions when reflecting on a semester, since they do not show up on a transcript, but they genuinely shaped how the event turned out for all of us.

Looking back, I think the exhibition taught me something that complemented what I was learning academically. Programming Fundamentals itself was teaching me how to think logically and solve problems in code. The exhibition taught me how to take that work and present it, collaborate around it, and be accountable to a team in a way that pure coursework rarely demands.

In the next post, I want to talk about how all of this academic and extracurricular effort ultimately came together in my Programming Fundamentals final results, and what that moment felt like after everything leading up to it.
