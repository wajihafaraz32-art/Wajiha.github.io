If I had to point to a single moment that changed how seriously I took my studies in that first semester, it would be the Programming Fundamentals midterm exam. Up until that point, I had a vague sense that I was struggling more than some of my classmates, but the midterm results made that gap impossible to ignore. My performance was average, and when I started comparing notes with classmates, it became clear that a number of them were performing noticeably better than I was.

I will not pretend the initial reaction to this was anything other than disappointment. There is a particular kind of discouragement that comes from putting in genuine effort and still seeing results that fall short of what you hoped for. I remember going over the exam afterward, trying to understand exactly where things had gone wrong, and feeling frustrated that the gaps in my understanding were so visible on paper, in a way that was harder to ignore than they had been during regular classwork.

For a short period after receiving those results, I found myself questioning my decision to pursue this field at all. It is easy, in hindsight, to say that one midterm does not define an entire academic journey, but in the moment, it genuinely felt significant. I think anyone who has gone through a similar experience, performing below their own expectations on an exam that felt important, will understand how disproportionately large that disappointment can feel at the time, even when, looking back later, it turns out to be a fairly normal part of the learning curve.

What mattered most, though, was what I chose to do with that disappointment. Instead of letting it convince me that I simply was not suited for programming, I made a deliberate decision to treat it as information rather than as a verdict. The midterm told me clearly where my understanding was weak, and rather than avoiding that discomfort, I decided to use it as a starting point for figuring out exactly what I needed to work on going forward.

This was not an instant shift in mindset. There were a few days where the disappointment lingered and made it hard to think constructively about next steps. But gradually, that initial discouragement turned into something more useful: a clearer sense of motivation. I started to see the midterm not as proof that I could not succeed in this subject, but as evidence that my current approach to studying needed to change if I wanted a different outcome by the time finals arrived.

Looking back now, I am genuinely glad this setback happened when it did, even though it did not feel that way at the time. It forced an honest reckoning with my study habits much earlier in the semester than I might have otherwise had, which gave me enough runway to actually make meaningful changes before the final exam.

In the next post, I want to talk about exactly what that change in approach looked like, since the period after the midterm became one of the more important stretches of my entire first semester.
