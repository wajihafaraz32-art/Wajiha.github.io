Assalam o Alaikum everyone. I wanted to start this blog by sharing something a little more personal than a typical project write-up: the story of my first two semesters as a Computer Engineering student, especially everything I went through in Programming Fundamentals and Database Management Systems. I am writing this not because it was a perfect journey, but because it absolutely was not, and I think that is exactly why it is worth writing down.

When I look back at the past year, academics were only one part of the picture. There was also the personal growth that came with leaving behind a familiar routine and stepping into something I did not fully understand yet. There was the discomfort of learning new skills from zero, the frustration of hitting walls I could not immediately climb over, and the slow, sometimes uneven process of adapting to a university environment that was nothing like the one I had imagined for myself in school. None of that shows up neatly in a transcript, but it shaped me just as much as any exam did.

I think a lot of students who are new to computer science assume that everyone around them already has some kind of head start, and in my case, that assumption was not entirely wrong. Many of my classmates had touched a keyboard with real intent long before I had. I had not. That gap was real, and pretending it was not there would have only made things harder for me later. So this series of posts is partly a record of how I tried to close that gap, semester by semester, subject by subject.

What I want this blog to be, more than anything, is honest. I do not want to write polished success stories where everything worked out the way I had planned, because that is not what happened. There were setbacks, disappointing midterms, long days as a commuting student, and a lot of moments where I doubted whether this field was really for me. But there were also small wins that meant a lot at the time: finally understanding a loop that had confused me for a week, finishing a project I thought was beyond me, and slowly realizing that I was capable of more than I gave myself credit for at the start.

I am sharing this publicly for a simple reason. As a Computer Engineering student, I think it matters to leave some kind of digital footprint, a record of how I was thinking and growing at this stage of my education. Years from now, I would like to be able to look back at these posts and see exactly where I started. And if even one other student who is feeling overwhelmed in their first semester reads this and feels less alone in it, that would mean something to me too.

Over the coming posts, I am going to walk through this journey roughly in the order it happened. I will talk about why I ended up in Computer Engineering in the first place, even though it was not my original plan. I will talk about my first day at university, the disorientation of it, and how that slowly turned into familiarity. I will go deep into my experience learning Programming Fundamentals, including the parts that genuinely frustrated me. I will talk about what it was like commuting as a day scholar while trying to keep up with coursework, and later, what it was like to move into the hostel and learn to live independently for the first time.

I will also spend real time on the two subjects that shaped this year the most: Programming Fundamentals and Database Management Systems. Both came with their own version of a midterm wake-up call, their own slow recovery, and their own final project that taught me more than the lectures alone ever could. The PF exhibition and the DBMS project, in particular, were turning points, not because I suddenly became an expert, but because I had to apply what I was learning in a way that felt real for the first time.

There were also moments outside of pure academics that mattered just as much. The welcome event, sports week, navigating Ramadan alongside a packed semester schedule, the support of classmates and a class representative who kept things organized when everything felt chaotic. University life is rarely just about the syllabus, and I want this blog to reflect that fuller picture rather than just the technical side of things.

By the end of this series, I hope to have captured something honest about what the first year of a Computer Engineering degree can look like for someone who started with very little background in the field. Not a highlight reel, but a real account of the effort it took, the people who helped along the way, and the lessons that only became clear in hindsight. I am still very much in the middle of this journey, with new challenges ahead, including subjects I am still a little nervous about. But I am hopeful, and I am motivated to keep learning.

Thank you for reading this introduction. In the next post, I will go back to where this all started: the decision that brought me into Computer Engineering in the first place, even though it was not where I originally saw myself heading.
