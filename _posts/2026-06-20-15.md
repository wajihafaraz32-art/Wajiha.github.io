As I bring this series of posts to a close, I want to step back from the individual events of the past two semesters and talk about what I actually take away from all of it, now that I have had some time to reflect on the full picture rather than just the moment-to-moment experience of living through it.

The clearest lesson from this entire journey is that success does not arrive without real difficulty along the way. Looking back across both Programming Fundamentals and Database Management Systems, I can point to specific moments of stress, confusion, and discouragement in nearly every stage of the process. The midterm setbacks in both subjects, the disorientation of starting university with little programming background, the exhaustion of commuting as a day scholar, the difficulty of adjusting to hostel life, all of these were genuinely hard at the time. None of them felt minor while I was living through them.

What I learned, gradually and not always gracefully, was the importance of not giving up when those difficulties showed up. This is easy to say in hindsight and much harder to actually practice in the moment, especially when a midterm result makes you question whether you belong in a field at all. But each time I chose to keep going rather than withdraw, whether that meant putting in extra practice after a disappointing exam or pushing through exhaustion to keep attending classes consistently, I ended up better off for it later, even if the improvement was not always immediately visible.

I also came to appreciate the role that hard work, consistency, and patience played throughout this journey, not as abstract virtues, but as practical habits that produced real, measurable differences in outcomes. The gap between my midterm and final performance in Programming Fundamentals, and the way the DBMS project ultimately came together after a rocky midterm in that subject too, both reinforced the same lesson: sustained effort over time tends to matter more than any single moment of struggle or success.

Alongside all of this, I found a quieter source of strength in my faith. There were moments, particularly during the more overwhelming stretches of my second semester, when I leaned on that faith to stay grounded and to keep perspective on what mattered. I think that combination, practical effort paired with a sense of faith and patience, is part of what got me through this year in one piece.

I also want to acknowledge that not every result from this period was something I was fully satisfied with, and that even now, there are still outcomes I am waiting on with some nervousness, including my Calculus result, which I am still a little worried about as I write this. I think it is important to end this series honestly rather than wrapping everything up too neatly. Not every part of this journey has resolved itself yet, and I am still very much in the middle of figuring things out.

Looking ahead, my goals are fairly straightforward. I want to keep improving my technical skills, particularly in areas where I know my foundation is still developing. I want to work toward stronger academic results going forward, building on the lessons I learned from both of my midterm setbacks this past year. And more broadly, I want to keep growing into a more confident Computer Engineer, not in the sense of having everything figured out, but in the sense of trusting that I can handle whatever difficulties come next, because I have already gotten through a year's worth of them.

Thank you for following along with this series. Writing it has been a useful exercise in its own right, forcing me to actually sit with and articulate experiences that, at the time, mostly just felt like things I had to get through. I am hopeful about what comes next, and I plan to keep documenting this journey as it continues.
